Agreeing on a purchase price before testing cash flow, assets and deal structure can leave a buyer with an unworkable funding gap. If you are asking what finance options exist for a business acquisition in the UK, several routes are available. These include senior debt, business loans, asset-backed lending, vendor finance, equity, management funding and blended structures.

## Start With the Deal, Not the Finance Product

The right finance structure starts with the target business and the proposed transaction. Affordability, trading performance, available security and your cash contribution will shape the available routes.

Start by separating the purchase price into its main components:
- Payment for goodwill and expected future earnings
- Payment for stock, equipment, machinery or other assets
- Payment for property or premises
- Professional fees and other transaction costs
- Working capital needed after completion
- Cash retained as a buffer against delayed payments or integration costs

Each component may need a different funding source. A suitable structure might combine buyer cash, senior debt, asset-backed funding and deferred consideration.

## How Acquisition Finance Differs From an Ordinary Business Loan

Acquisition finance is funding raised specifically to purchase a business or its assets. The lender assesses the transaction and the combined business after completion.

A general [business loan](https://fundingbay.co.uk/business-loans/) may fund working capital, growth or asset purchases. Funding Bay brokers business funding for working capital, growth and asset purchases, subject to each lender's criteria.

A business loan does not automatically suit an acquisition. The lender must understand the purchase price, target performance, funding structure and post-completion repayment position.

### What Lenders Are Funding

An acquisition lender funds a defined transaction rather than a preferred facility size. The lender considers how the purchase creates a viable combined business.

Debt-service capacity means the cash flow available to meet capital and interest repayments. It depends on sustainable earnings, working-capital movements, tax, investment requirements and existing debt commitments.

A lender may fund the purchase price, certain transaction costs or part of the post-completion cash requirement. Eligible uses depend on the facility, lender criteria and legal structure.

The proposed borrower also matters. Funding may sit within the buyer, the target or a new holding company established for the acquisition.

### Why Deal Structure Matters

Acquisition debt can be secured against the target's assets, the buyer's existing assets or both. Security gives a lender rights over specified assets if repayment obligations remain unpaid.

Lenders can also require financial covenants, forecasts and a detailed integration plan. A covenant is a contractual condition within the finance agreement. It can require the borrower to maintain agreed financial measures or provide regular reporting.

The legal structure affects guarantees, security priority, tax treatment and access to target cash flow. Obtain transaction-specific advice before signing purchase or funding documents.

### Senior Debt and Business Loans

Secured and unsecured business loans can provide a lump sum towards an acquisition. This works where the target produces enough cash to meet scheduled repayments.

**Typical Structure:** Secured borrowing uses agreed business assets as security. Unsecured borrowing does not take asset security, although a lender may request a personal guarantee.

Funding Bay describes [unsecured loan facilities](https://fundingbay.co.uk/business-loans-unsecured-business-loans/) from £1,000–£500,000, with fixed interest rates and terms of one to three years. Acquisition suitability remains subject to affordability, lender criteria and the transaction structure.

**Who This Fits:** This suits established buyers or targets with consistent earnings, clear forecasts and manageable existing debt.

This does not fit where repayments would absorb the cash needed for stock, payroll, integration or seasonal trading. Compare the proposed term with the target's cash generation cycle.

Prepare a debt-service calculation before approaching lenders. Include existing commitments, acquisition repayments and a reasonable post-completion cash buffer.

### Asset-Backed Lending and Asset Finance

Asset-backed lending connects borrowing capacity to identifiable business assets. Relevant assets can include receivables, stock, equipment, machinery or property.

**Typical Structure:** The lender takes security over specified assets. The facility may depend on asset eligibility, valuation, ownership and existing finance arrangements.

[Asset finance](https://fundingbay.co.uk/asset-finance/) funds new assets or raises capital against eligible existing assets. Funding Bay arranges asset finance options for equipment, machinery and other business assets.

**Typical Structure:** Hire purchase supports eventual ownership, while leasing provides use for an agreed period. Asset refinance and sale and leaseback can release cash from existing assets.

**Who This Fits:** This works well where the target has a detailed asset register, clear ownership records and assets with ongoing operational value.

Asset-backed facilities do not automatically cover goodwill. Check whether asset funding supports the purchase price, post-completion working capital or both.

List each asset, its owner, current finance and expected role after completion. Ask the funder to confirm what can support the proposed facility.

### Vendor Finance and Deferred Consideration

Vendor finance leaves part of the agreed purchase price payable to the seller after completion. Deferred consideration is a broader term for purchase payments made at a later date.

**Typical Structure:** A seller loan places part of the price on agreed repayment terms. An earn-out links later payments to specified future performance targets.

These structures require clear legal documents covering payment dates, interest, performance measures and dispute procedures. They must also address the seller's position behind senior debt.

**Who This Fits:** This suits transactions where the seller accepts staged payment and remains confident in future performance.

This does not remove execution risk. An earn-out can create disagreement if accounting policies, customer losses or integration decisions affect the agreed targets.

Ask an [SRA-regulated solicitor](https://www.sra.org.uk/) to document the seller's rights and payment conditions. Confirm that senior lenders accept the proposed repayment priority.

### Equity Investment

Equity provides capital in return for an ownership interest. It can reduce scheduled debt commitments during the integration period.

**Typical Structure:** The investor contributes cash and receives shares. The investment agreement can include voting rights, information rights, reserved matters and exit provisions.

Equity has no scheduled debt repayment. The commercial cost includes dilution and the investor's influence over future decisions.

**Who This Fits:** This suits acquisitions needing patient capital, sector expertise or more funding than the target can responsibly borrow.

This does not fit buyers who cannot accept shared ownership or investor controls. Agree decision rights and future funding expectations before completing the transaction.

Prepare an investment case covering valuation, management capability, growth plans and exit expectations. Obtain legal and tax advice on the proposed share structure.

### Management Buyouts, Management Buy-Ins and Blended Funding

A management buyout, or MBO, is a purchase led by the target's existing management team. A management buy-in, or MBI, is led by an incoming team.

**Typical Structure:** Either transaction can combine management capital, senior debt, asset-backed lending, vendor support and external equity.

Existing managers may understand customers, staff and operational risks. Incoming managers must show how their sector experience supports continuity and growth.

**Who This Fits:** An MBO suits an established management team taking ownership from existing shareholders. An MBI suits experienced buyers entering a target they do not currently manage.

A blended structure divides the requirement among several funding sources. It can match long-term acquisition debt with shorter-term working-capital support.

**Typical Structure:** Buyer cash absorbs part of the risk. Debt funds an affordable portion, while seller support or equity fills an agreed funding gap.

**Who This Fits:** This works where one facility cannot responsibly cover the price, fees and post-completion cash requirement.

Set out each layer in a sources-and-uses schedule. Confirm the order of security, repayment and distributions before signing finance documents.

### Government-Backed Lending

The [Growth Guarantee Scheme](https://fundingbay.co.uk/growth-guarantee-scheme/) can support eligible lending through term loans, overdrafts, asset finance or invoice finance. It does not automatically fund every acquisition.

**Typical Structure:** An accredited lender provides the facility and completes its own underwriting. The borrower remains responsible for the debt.

Borrowers are not required to use their principal private residence as security. Lenders can still apply other security requirements and request guarantees under their own criteria.

**Who This Fits:** This can suit an eligible UK trading business that meets current lender and scheme requirements.

Check Growth Guarantee Scheme finance against the proposed borrower, transaction purpose and facility type. Confirm eligibility before including it within your funding plan.

## Match the Finance to the Target Assets

Separate purchase-price funding from the facilities needed after completion. This prevents working-capital requirements from becoming an unexpected funding gap.

Map the transaction using these prompts:
- Match goodwill to sustainable earnings, buyer capital, equity or deferred consideration.
- Match unpaid customer invoices to receivables-based working-capital facilities.
- Match equipment and machinery to asset finance or asset refinance.
- Match stock requirements to cash flow, asset-backed lending or buyer contribution.
- Match property to suitable secured borrowing, subject to valuation and legal review.
- Match fees and integration costs to committed cash sources.
- Keep an identified cash buffer for seasonal trading and delayed customer payments.

### Goodwill and Future Earnings

Goodwill represents value beyond separately identifiable physical assets. It may include customer relationships, reputation, systems and expected future earnings.

Goodwill is not a separately identifiable security asset like equipment or receivables. A lender therefore considers whether sustainable cash flow can support borrowing linked to that value.

Forecasts should connect purchase assumptions with expected cash generation. They should also show customer concentration, recurring revenue and likely integration costs.

Consider buyer cash, senior debt, equity or deferred consideration for the goodwill element. The suitable mix depends on affordability and the wider transaction.

### Stock, Debtors and Equipment

Stock may contribute to an asset-backed facility where a lender accepts its type, ownership and saleability. Existing security or retention-of-title claims can affect its value.

A debtor book can support post-completion working capital through [invoice finance](https://fundingbay.co.uk/invoice-finance/). This can release cash tied up in unpaid invoices and may be quicker to establish than traditional bank lending.

[Selective invoice finance](https://fundingbay.co.uk/invoice-finance-selective-invoice-finance/) can fund chosen invoices, projects or debtors. It remains a working-capital tool rather than an automatic method for financing goodwill.

Equipment can support hire purchase, leasing or refinance. The choice depends on whether you want ownership, temporary use or cash released from an existing asset.

Asset refinance, hire purchase and leasing options have different ownership and repayment implications. Sale and leaseback can also release cash while the business keeps using the asset.

### Property and Premises

Property can provide relevant security when premises form part of the transaction. The lender will consider valuation, ownership, existing charges and the property's commercial use.

Separate the property requirement from the operating business purchase. This helps you identify which cash flows service each facility.

Check whether the property sits within the target company or another entity. Your solicitor should review title, charges, leases and transaction structure.

Allow time for valuation and legal work within the completion timetable. Avoid assuming that property security removes the need for affordability assessment.

## What Lenders Will Assess Before They Fund

Lenders assess the buyer, target and transaction together. A strong target can still face funding problems if the structure or timetable is unclear.

### The Buyer, Target and Transaction

The lender will examine the purchase price and its valuation rationale. It will compare that price with historic performance, assets and management projections.

The buyer must show relevant experience, available cash and a credible integration plan. Lenders also consider existing commitments and the proposed ownership structure.

The target assessment includes historic accounts, current management information, cash flow, customer concentration and available security. Forecasts must show how the combined business will meet repayments.

A personal guarantee is a director's contractual promise to meet specified business borrowing obligations if the company cannot. Lenders do not require one in every case.

The transaction timetable must allow for due diligence, credit assessment, valuation and legal documentation. A rushed completion date can limit suitable funding routes.

## Your Acquisition Readiness Checklist

Use this checklist to organise the deal before approaching lenders. Requirements vary, but each item answers an affordability, security or execution question.

1.  **Agreed or proposed purchase price** State the amount, payment timing and any conditional elements.
2.  **Valuation rationale** Explain how earnings, assets and commercial factors support the proposed price.
3.  **Last three years of target accounts where available** Show historic revenue, profit, cash generation and balance-sheet movements.
4.  **Current management accounts** Provide a recent view of trading since the latest filed accounts.
5.  **Management forecast** Show sales, margins, working capital, investment needs and debt repayments after completion.
6.  **Buyer CVs and sector experience** Explain who will operate the business and why the team can deliver the plan.
7.  **Debt schedule** List current lenders, balances, repayments, security and settlement requirements.
8.  **Asset register** Identify equipment, machinery, vehicles, property and existing finance.
9.  **Aged debtor and creditor reports** Show customer payment patterns, overdue balances and supplier obligations.
10.  **Bank statements** Support the current cash position, trading activity and buyer contribution.
11.  **Proposed security** Identify assets that may support funding and any existing charges.
12.  **Buyer cash contribution** Confirm the amount, source and timing of available cash.
13.  **Seller terms** Record deferred consideration, earn-outs, seller loans and repayment expectations.
14.  **Realistic completion timetable** Allow enough time for underwriting, due diligence, valuation and legal work.

Keep assumptions consistent across the business plan, forecast and funding request. Differences between documents can delay lender assessment.

## Build a Funding Stack That Protects Cash Flow

A low headline rate does not make a facility suitable. Repayment timing can place more pressure on working capital than the total cost suggests.

### Compare Costs, Fees and Repayments

Compare the total borrowing cost across the expected term. Review the interest basis, arrangement fees, legal costs, valuation costs and other facility charges.

Examine when repayments begin and how often they fall due. Check whether the profile matches customer payment cycles, seasonal trading and integration spending.

Review early repayment terms, security, covenants and personal guarantees. Ask what happens if performance falls below the forecast.

Deferred consideration may need to rank behind senior debt. This means senior lender obligations receive priority under the agreed documents.

Build a monthly cash flow that includes every funding layer. The model should show capital repayments, interest, seller payments and essential operational spending.

### Short Glossary

**Acquisition Finance Glossary**
- **Acquisition finance:** Debt or equity raised specifically to purchase a business or its assets.
- **MBO:** A business purchase led by the target's existing management team.
- **MBI:** A business purchase led by an incoming management team.
- **Vendor finance:** Purchase funding provided by the seller.
- **Deferred consideration:** Part of the purchase price paid after completion.
- **Earn-out:** A later payment linked to agreed future performance targets.

- **Covenant:** A contractual condition that the borrower must observe.
- **Personal guarantee:** A director's promise to meet specified company obligations if the company cannot.

### Stress-Test the Post-Completion Position

Build a downside case before committing to the purchase. Test lower sales, slower customer payments, margin pressure, integration costs and delayed synergies.

The model should show the lowest expected cash position under each scenario. Include payroll, tax, supplier payments, investment and existing debt obligations.

Assess what management can change if performance falls below plan. Possible actions include delaying discretionary spending or adjusting the integration timetable.

If target cash flow cannot support the proposed debt, revisit the structure. Review the price, buyer contribution, repayment profile, deferred consideration and completion timing.

More borrowing should not become the default answer. A smaller debt burden can preserve working capital and reduce pressure during integration.

## The Bottom Line

The right acquisition structure follows the target's cash flow, assets, buyer contribution and transaction timetable. The largest available facility is rarely a useful decision target.

Start with the purchase components and post-completion cash requirement. Match each requirement to an earnings stream, asset, seller term or buyer contribution.

Further borrowing may not be appropriate where the buyer or target is distressed. It also does not fit where the combined business cannot support repayments.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I finance a business acquisition without a proven trading history?**
It can be possible where the target has established trading performance and the management team has relevant experience. Expect lenders to examine your contribution, forecasts, security and the target's ability to service debt.

**Can acquisition finance cover transaction fees as well as the purchase price?**
Some facilities can include approved transaction costs, subject to lender criteria and affordability. Identify legal, valuation and advisory fees separately, then confirm which costs each funding source permits.

**What should a new holding company provide when seeking acquisition finance?**
Provide its ownership structure, constitutional documents, directors and source of buyer funds. The lender will also need target financial information and details of how cash reaches the holding company for repayments.

**What happens if the target company has historic adverse credit?**
Historic adverse credit can narrow the available routes without ending the assessment. Lenders will consider its cause, age, current status and impact on affordability, while specialist bad-credit funding criteria may differ.
